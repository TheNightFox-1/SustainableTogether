# MRV for living-system regeneration

Measurement, reporting and verification of regenerative outcomes in living systems. R02 names
this "the binding constraint" on the whole field: biochar permanence, soil carbon accrual,
enhanced-weathering dissolution rates, ocean-alkalinity air–sea equilibration, peatland
CH₄/CO₂ trade-offs, kelp-sinking decomposition and farmer-managed natural regeneration
additionality all fail in the same place — outcome verification at adequate spatial and
temporal resolution, at acceptable cost [R02 gap analysis §1].

## Why this is the WG's opening

R02 states that this frontier "is largely absent from MBSE/SysML-friendly engineering
literature" [R02 gap analysis §1]. That is a gap named by a sustainability review, addressed
to exactly the competence this WG has.

Two adjacent gaps in the same analysis point the same way: coupled hydro-bio-geochemical
modelling linking Indigenous-managed flows to formal hydrological and ecological models is
nascent [R02 gap analysis §2], and governance pathways — secured land and sea tenure above
all — are "the single most decisive enabler of regenerative outcomes, yet are rarely modelled
as system parameters" [R02 gap analysis §7].

R02's own modelling triage is the honest constraint on any such programme: solutions amenable
to MBSE need quantifiable mass/energy/information flows, clear boundaries with definable
interfaces, and discrete formalisable parts. Anaerobic digestion, managed aquifer recharge and
constructed wetlands qualify; rewilding trajectories, commons governance and story-of-place
do not [R02 MBSE addendum].

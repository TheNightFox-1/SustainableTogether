# Net positive

The claim that a firm's total effect on the systems it touches is restorative rather than
merely less harmful. R03 uses it as the threshold that separates regenerative business models
from circular ones: circular strategies close, narrow and slow resource loops, but mainly
address harm reduction, and "can only go so far in creating a sustainable economy"
[R03 pp. 529–530, citing Bocken et al. 2016 and Allwood 2014].

R12 gives the term operational structure without using the label, splitting business impact
along two axes — direct/indirect and negative/positive — into four moves: eliminate your own
negative impact (obligatory), create positive impact yourself, reduce others' negative impact,
and amplify others' positive impact [R12]. Only the first is a requirement in that scheme;
the other three are how a firm goes beyond break-even.

## Why the word "net" is the problem

Netting requires a common denominator, and the corpus is explicit that the denominator is the
open question. [Sustainability context](sustainability-context.md) records R11's argument that
a figure without its threshold answers "compared to what?" with nothing [R11 slides 20–21].
[Tri-profit](tri-profit.md) records R04's alternative: keep the three contexts in their own
units and refuse the common currency [R04 FP4]. And [rebound effect](rebound-effect.md)
records what happens when the system boundary is drawn tightly enough to make a net figure
look good [R09 pp. 8–9].

A net-positive claim in a WG document should therefore always be accompanied by its system
boundary and its threshold. [WG]

## Contested

R03 treats net positive as the goal state; R11's threshold argument implies no net figure is
interpretable without an absolute reference; R12 makes the absolute reference (break-even
goals) mandatory and the net-positive part voluntary. These are compatible in principle and
are stated at different levels of rigour in practice.

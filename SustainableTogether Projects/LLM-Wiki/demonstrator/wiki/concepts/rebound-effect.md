# Rebound effect

An improvement at component level that is cancelled, or reversed, by the response of the
system it sits in. R09's opening examples are the canonical shape: optimising car engines
while overall fuel consumption rises, and battery production shifting impact into mining
[R09 p. 4].

## Why it belongs in a regeneration wiki

Because it is the mechanism by which a regenerative claim fails without anyone lying.

R09's worked case is a second-hand resale platform whose commissioned study reported
453 ktCO₂eq avoided, or 1.8 kgCO₂eq per purchase [R09 pp. 6–7, citing Vaayu 2021]. Setting the
system boundary wider changes the picture: additional impacts from impulse buying while
browsing, from parcel delivery and platform operations, from how sellers spend the money, and
from the decline in charities' social activity as donations drop. R09 marks these as
considered, partially considered, and not considered in the original figure [R09 pp. 8–9].
The conclusion it draws is not that the study is wrong but that the figure "does not help
designers to improve the socioenvironmental impacts" [R09 p. 9].

R02 records the same failure mode from the LCA side, naming Zink & Geyer's "Circular Economy
Rebound" alongside Bjørn & Hauschild and Tukker, and concluding that regenerative claims
"often fail when subjected to dynamic, system-boundary-corrected LCA" [R02 gap analysis §3].

R07 is the constructive answer: guidelines for embedding systems-thinking principles into
early-stage sustainability assessment tools, for the stage where quantitative data, time and
competence are all scarce [R07 abstract].

## Contested

None. Three independent sources, three literatures, one mechanism.

# Regeneration

The corpus does not hold one definition of regeneration. It holds three, from three
communities, and they are not interchangeable. This page keeps them apart on purpose.

**Design tradition.** Regeneration is what a design does when it works with the living
systems of a specific place rather than on them: designs that "regenerate rather than
deplete underlying life support systems and resources within socio-ecological wholes"
[R01 glossary]. The unit of analysis is a place, and the aim is a system that becomes
self-organizing and self-evolving [R01 glossary].

**Business tradition.** Regeneration is a property of a firm's model: business models that
"actively restore the natural and social systems they operate in, creating a more holistic
net positive impact" [R03 p.529]. The unit of analysis is the enterprise, and the test is
evidenced, continuing activity rather than stated intent [R03 p.533].

**Engineering usage.** In engineering vocabulary the word is mostly efficiency-coded —
regenerative braking, regenerative thermal oxidizers, waste-heat recovery — recovering
exergy or extending product life rather than generating anything. Generative function
proper (net primary production, soil formation, hydrological replenishment) overwhelmingly
depends on living systems [R02 exec. summary].

## Why the distinction matters to us

A WG document that says "regenerative" without saying which of the three it means will be
read three different ways by three different readers. It is also the fault line under
[the sustainability definition question](../questions/q01-which-sustainability.md): the design
tradition treats regeneration as a replacement for sustainability-as-harm-reduction
[R01 §II], while the business tradition treats it as the next step beyond net zero
[R03 p.529].

## Contested

- **Scope of the term.** R01 anchors regeneration in place-based, co-evolutionary practice;
  R03 anchors it in firm-level strategy; R02 records the engineering usage as a third,
  largely unrelated sense. No source in the corpus reconciles the three. Open as
  [Q02](../questions/q02-regeneration-scope.md).
- **Whether "regenerative" survives measurement.** R02 reports that regenerative claims
  "often fail when subjected to dynamic, system-boundary-corrected LCA", naming Zink & Geyer's
  circular-economy rebound and Chaplot & Smith (2023) [R02 gap analysis §3]. This is the same
  failure mode [rebound effect](rebound-effect.md) describes at product level.
- **Whether regeneration is modellable at all.** R02's own MBSE addendum places "regenerative
  business models", permaculture, regenerative leadership and story-of-place in its **weak fit**
  category — essential to the field, resistant to formal modelling — while placing
  regenerative braking, anaerobic digestion and managed aquifer recharge in **strong fit**
  [R02 MBSE addendum]. The category the WG most wants to model is the one the review says
  resists modelling.

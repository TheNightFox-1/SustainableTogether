# Sustainability context

The principle that a performance figure is meaningless without the ecological or social
threshold it is measured against. R11 tracks the idea back through Donella Meadows' work on
indicators, the 1972 *Limits to Growth* treatment of carrying capacity and overshoot, and
Catton's "phantom carrying capacity" — capacity that exists only while temporarily available
resources last [R11 slides 8–10]. Its lineage runs further back than the reporting standards
that dropped it: R11 takes the argument to Plato's *Laws* Book V and the Plimsoll line
[R11 slide 7].

The measurement consequence, in R11's framing, is that sustainability measurement needs both
a numerator and a denominator — a quantity of impact *and* the threshold it is set against.
Reporting that gives only the numerator answers the question "compared to what?" with nothing
[R11 slides 20–21].

Thresholds research supplies candidate denominators: planetary boundaries [R11 slide 17,
citing Rockström et al. 2009] and the doughnut's ecological ceiling with a social foundation
[R11 slide 17, citing Raworth 2012].

[The Future-Fit Business Benchmark](../entities/future-fit-business-benchmark.md) is the
corpus's clearest worked example of the principle applied to a firm: its break-even goals are
absolute conditions a company must meet to stop hindering progress, not year-on-year
improvements against its own past [R12].

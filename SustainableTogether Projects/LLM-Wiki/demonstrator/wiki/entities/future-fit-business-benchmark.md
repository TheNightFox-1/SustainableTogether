# Future-Fit Business Benchmark

A threshold-based benchmark from the Future-Fit Foundation, presented to INCOSE in February
2026 by Martin Rich. Its target state: "A Future-Fit Society will protect the possibility that
humans and other life can flourish on Earth forever" — environmentally restorative, socially
just, economically inclusive [R12].

## Structure

**Break-even goals** — what every company must do to avoid slowing society's progress. R12
lists them across eight areas: energy, water, waste, pollution, natural resources, people,
presence and drivers. Examples: operations emit no greenhouse gases; operational waste is
eliminated; employees are paid at least a living wage; the right tax is paid in the right
place at the right time; lobbying and corporate influence safeguard the pursuit of
future-fitness [R12].

**Positive pursuits** — optional, and framed as three distinct moves beyond break-even:
create positive impact yourself, reduce others' negative impact, amplify others' positive
impact [R12].

R12 sorts corporate responses into defensive (shareholder value), selective (shared value),
and holistic (system value), and places the benchmark squarely in the third [R12].

## Why it matters here

It is the corpus's cleanest example of [sustainability context](../concepts/sustainability-context.md)
made operational — absolute conditions rather than year-on-year improvement against a firm's
own past. R04 independently locates "future-fit business" near the strong end of its
continuum of definitions of business success [R04].

## Contested

Not contested in the corpus. Note the status limit: R12 is a slide deck, so break-even goal
wording here is as presented, not as specified in the benchmark's own documentation. The
benchmark specification is not in the raw registry.

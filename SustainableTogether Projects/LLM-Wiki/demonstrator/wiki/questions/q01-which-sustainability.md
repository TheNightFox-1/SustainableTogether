# Which definition of sustainability does the WG use?

Raised automatically during ingest of R11, against claims already carried by
[strong sustainability](../concepts/strong-sustainability.md) from R04 and R10.

## The fork

**(a) Three pillars, no thresholds.** Sustainability as a system characteristic and an
enterprise ethic, described through environmental, economic and social dimensions
[R10 slides 3–7]. This is the framing an INCOSE audience already has, and it aligns with
Vision 2035's language.

**(b) Thresholds required.** Sustainability performance is uninterpretable without the
ecological ceiling or social floor it is measured against; standards that dropped the
Sustainability Context principle dropped sustainability with it [R11 slides 4–24].

R04 supplies the vocabulary that names the fork — weak versus strong sustainability, turning
on whether capitals may be substituted [R04, citing Neumayer 2013 pp. 26–28] — but does not
settle it for a systems engineering audience.

## Why it cannot be deferred

Every definition in the compendium inherits from this one. [Net positive](../concepts/net-positive.md),
[tri-profit](../concepts/tri-profit.md) and [regeneration](../concepts/regeneration.md) all
resolve differently under (a) and (b). Writing them before deciding means writing them twice.

## What would settle it

- A WG position statement, or
- an explicit two-level compendium: the INCOSE-facing definition and the threshold-based
  critique of it, held side by side and labelled as such. [WG]

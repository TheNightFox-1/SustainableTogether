# Impact Model — Regeneration in PV (Desired State)

**Version:** 0.1 (2026-07-04)
**Status:** Initial (to be validated through DS-II)

## What This Represents

The Impact Model is a graphical representation of the **desired future state** — how things should be after introducing the regenerative design support. It shows what changes when the regenerative methodology is applied.

For our research, the Impact Model captures the SustainaSun state — a circular, regenerative PV system with integrated ecological, social, and financial outcomes.

## Changes from Reference to Impact

### Natural Capital: DEGENERATIVE → REGENERATIVE
- **SOIL:** Conventional → agrivoltaic management, soil carbon increasing
- **BIO:** Mono-use → habitat management, species richness increasing
- **WATER:** Standard → enhanced retention, infiltration
- **GHG:** ~20-30 g/kWh → <15 g/kWh (verified EPD)

### Social Capital: MINIMAL → ACTIVE
- **COMMUNITY:** Transactional → co-ownership, trust-building
- **WEALTH:** Extracted → retained locally (community equity)
- **ACCESS:** Market-priced → social tariffs, designated access

### Financial Capital: NARROW → DIVERSIFIED
- **REVENUE:** Electricity only → electricity + low-carbon premium + biodiversity credits + community equity WACC reduction + circularity savings
- **COSTS:** Standard → higher upfront (ecological management, DfD) but lower risk
- **INVESTOR:** Conventional IRR → risk-adjusted IRR (lower risk from diversification + community support)

### Manufactured Capital: LINEAR → CIRCULAR
- **PRODUCT:** Conventional → designed for disassembly + ecological integration
- **CIRCULARITY:** 50-70% → 85%+ high-value mass recovery
- **SUPPLY-CHAIN:** High carbon → supplier decarbonization, low-carbon sourcing

## The Upward Helix (Reinforcing Loops)

```
R1: SOIL HEALTH → land productivity → agrivoltaic revenue → reinvestment → SOIL HEALTH
R2: COMMUNITY TRUST → permitting speed → project scale → community benefit → TRUST
R3: LOW-CARBON PREMIUM → sourcing pressure → supplier decarbonization → better EPD → HIGHER PREMIUM
R4: INSTALLED BASE → EOL returns → reverse logistics → material bank → LEASING-COST REDUCTION
```

Each loop is an upward helix (Fischer 2024): partly self-perpetuating, but needs ongoing input.

## Balancing Loops (Limits)

```
B1: Compliance overhead (cost of MRV, certification)
B2: Market saturation (biodiversity credit market immaturity)
B3: Natural limits (soil type limits, habitat carrying capacity)
B4: Regulatory change (WEEE take-back, carbon pricing)
```

## Net Impact

The Impact Model shows that regeneration is **not just "less bad"** — it's a qualitatively different system:
- **Financial:** diversified revenue, lower risk, potential for higher risk-adjusted returns
- **Ecological:** active restoration, not just mitigation
- **Social:** co-creation, not just mitigation
- **Product:** circular, not linear

## Validation Required (DS-II)

This is the *intended* impact. DS-II will test:
- Which reinforcing loops actually compound?
- Where do balancing loops cap the helix?
- Under what conditions does the impact materialise?

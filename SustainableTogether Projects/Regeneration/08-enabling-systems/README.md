# Group 3 — Enabling Systems

> **New group (2026-08-09):** Study the systems, infrastructure, and conditions that *enable* the regenerative PV system and business model to function in practice.

---

## Mandate

The PV system and the PVaaS business model cannot be regenerative in isolation. They need **enabling systems** — the policy, market, infrastructure, governance, and socio-technical conditions that make regeneration operational rather than theoretical.

This group studies those enabling systems.

---

## Why this matters

You can design a perfect regenerative business model on paper, but if:
- There's no regulatory pathway for biodiversity credits,
- The grid operator won't accommodate agrivoltaic land use,
- The supply chain has no low-carbon module manufacturers,
- There's no community governance structure for wealth sharing,
- The standards don't recognize regenerative land management as a credit-generating activity,

...then the model doesn't just have a gap — it has a *non-starter*.

**The question:** What systems need to exist (or be created) for this to work in reality?

---

## Scope

Study each enabling system across these dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Existence** | Does it already exist? If yes, what's the gap? |
| **Adaptability** | Can it be adapted from its current form? |
| **Creation cost** | What would it cost to build from scratch? |
| **Time horizon** | When could it be operational? |
| **Dependency** | Is it a hard blocker or a soft enabler? |

---

## Candidate enabling systems

These are the initial categories to study — the list should grow as analysis reveals more:

**1. Policy & Regulatory**
- Agrivoltaic zoning / dual-use land permits
- Biodiversity credit schemes (EU Biodiversity Credit scheme)
- Carbon credit / carbon farming frameworks
- Community renewable energy regulations
- Environmental impact assessment for regenerative land management

**2. Market & Financial Infrastructure**
- Low-carbon premium market (how is it priced, verified, traded?)
- Biodiversity credit markets (if/when they emerge)
- Community finance vehicles (energy cooperatives, community equity)
- Green bonds / sustainability-linked loans for regenerative projects
- Insurance products for regenerative risk

**3. Supply Chain & Materials**
- Low-carbon PV module manufacturers (EPD-verified TOPCon/HJT)
- EOL recycling capacity (FRELP/ROSI-class processes)
- Local/regional manufacturing capacity
- Sustainable mounting system suppliers

**4. Grid & Infrastructure**
- Grid connection requirements for dual-use PV
- Battery/storage integration policies
- Smart grid / demand-response participation
- Local energy trading / peer-to-peer frameworks

**5. Standards & Certification**
- Standards for regenerative land management (verification, audit)
- Integration of social outcomes into existing sustainability frameworks
- MRV standards that bridge ecological + financial reporting
- Alignment with EU Taxonomy, CSRD, CSDDD

**6. Governance & Social Infrastructure**
- Community energy governance models (cooperatives, shared ownership)
- Multi-stakeholder governance (farmers, energy company, community, regulators)
- Benefit-sharing mechanisms (who gets what, how is it decided)
- Monitoring and verification capacity at local level

---

## Method

For each enabling system:

1. **Map the current state** — what exists today in the relevant market(s)
2. **Identify the gap** — what's missing for our specific use case
3. **Classify** — hard blocker / soft enabler / optional amplifier
4. **Map dependencies** — which other enabling systems does it depend on?
5. **Assess feasibility** — cost, time, political difficulty to create/adapt
6. **Recommend** — what the Task-Force needs to plan for or build

---

## Output to other groups

- **To Group 1 (Business Model):** which revenue lines depend on which enabling systems (so the BM doesn't assume something that doesn't exist)
- **To Group 2 (System Dynamics):** which enabling systems create reinforcing loops (e.g., more regenerative projects → stronger biodiversity credit market → more regenerative projects)
- **To Group 3 (Product):** which supply chain constraints shape the system design

---

## Deliverables

1. **Enabling systems map** — the full landscape of what's needed
2. **Gap analysis** — per system: exists / gap / needs creation
3. **Dependency map** — which enabling systems depend on which others
4. **Feasibility assessment** — realistic timeline and barriers per system
5. **Critical path** — the minimum set of enabling systems that must exist for the model to work

---

## Key question to answer

> **"Which of these enabling systems are blockers, which are accelerators, and which can we skip and still have a viable regenerative model?"**

This is the honesty check that prevents the thesis from collapsing on assumptions about the outside world.

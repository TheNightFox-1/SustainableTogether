## 1. Purpose

Study the **enabling systems** — the policy, market, infrastructure, governance, and socio-technical conditions — that must exist (or be created) for the regenerative PV system and business model to function in practice. This is the reality check: a perfect model on paper is meaningless if the real world cannot support it.

## 2. The problem this group solves

You can design a regenerative PVaaS business model with positive NPV, reinforcing ecological loops, and community wealth creation — but if:
- There is no regulatory pathway for dual-use (agrivoltaic) land permits,
- Biodiversity credits don't exist or can't be verified,
- The supply chain has no low-carbon module manufacturers,
- The grid operator won't accommodate the operational model,
- No governance structure exists for community benefit sharing,

…then the model has a **non-starter**, not just a gap.

This group systematically maps every such dependency, classifies it, and answers:

> **Which enabling systems are blockers, which are accelerators, and which can we skip and still have a viable regenerative model?**

Conditions failing any of the three are published in a second list — *claimed but unverified*. See the roll-up rule in [`../RQ-DECOMPOSITION.md`](../RQ-DECOMPOSITION.md#roll-up-rule-for-rq2).

## 4. Scope

**In scope**
- Systematic mapping of all enabling systems across six categories (see below)
- For each: current-state assessment, gap identification, dependency mapping, feasibility assessment
- Classification as **hard blocker** / **soft enabler** / **optional amplifier**
- Identification of the **critical path** — the minimum set of enabling systems required for viability
- Identification of **enabling-system feedback loops** — where one enabling system creates conditions for another (e.g., stronger biodiversity credit market → more regenerative projects → stronger biodiversity credit market)

**Out of scope** (owned by other groups — do not do here)
- Building the business model or financial model → **Group 1, Business Model**.
- Formalising the product architecture → **Group 2, Product Regeneration**.
- The lifecycle environmental analysis → **Group 3, LCA & Financial**.
- Simulating feedback loops over time → **Group 4, System Dynamics**.
- Writing policy advocacy or lobbying → this group *identifies* enabling conditions; it does not campaign for them (see top-level "Scope & boundaries").

## 5. The six categories of enabling systems

### Category 1 — Policy & Regulatory

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Agrivoltaic zoning / dual-use land permits | Is there a legal pathway to combine energy generation and agriculture on the same land? |
| Biodiversity credit schemes | Do biodiversity credits exist (e.g., EU Biodiversity Credit scheme)? Can regenerative land management generate them? |
| Carbon farming / soil carbon frameworks | Are there recognised frameworks for soil carbon accounting and crediting? |
| Community renewable energy regulations | Does EU Community Renewable Energy Directive (2023) support the PVaaS/community equity model? |
| Environmental impact assessment | Can regenerative land management be recognised in EIA for net-positive impact? |

### Category 2 — Market & Financial Infrastructure

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Low-carbon premium market | Is there a market mechanism (certification, price differential, regulation) that translates lower lifecycle emissions into revenue? |
| Biodiversity credit markets | Do they exist? At what scale? At what price? Is the market liquid? |
| Community finance vehicles | Are there legal/financial instruments (energy cooperatives, community equity funds) that enable local wealth retention? |
| Green bonds / sustainability-linked loans | Can regenerative PV finance itself through these instruments at competitive rates? |
| Insurance products | Are there insurance products covering regenerative risk (e.g., biodiversity performance, community engagement)? |

### Category 3 — Supply Chain & Materials

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Low-carbon PV module manufacturers | Are there EPD-verified TOPCon/HJT producers with significantly lower manufacturing emissions? |
| EOL recycling capacity | Is there FRELP/ROSI-class recycling infrastructure at the project location? At what cost? |
| Local/regional manufacturing capacity | Can supply chain be regionalised for circularity? |
| Sustainable mounting system suppliers | Are there suppliers of reversible, low-impact mounting systems compatible with dual-use? |

### Category 4 — Grid & Infrastructure

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Grid connection for dual-use PV | Are there special grid rules for agrivoltaic/dual-use installations? |
| Battery / storage integration policies | Does the regulatory framework support behind-the-meter or community-scale storage? |
| Smart grid / demand-response | Can the system participate in demand-response markets? |
| Local energy trading / P2P | Are peer-to-peer energy trading frameworks in place? |

### Category 5 — Standards & Certification

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Regenerative land management standards | Are there verifiable, audited standards for regenerative land management? |
| Social outcomes in sustainability frameworks | Do CSRD, SFDR, EU Taxonomy cover the social outcomes we claim? |
| MRV standards bridging ecology + finance | Is there a recognised standard for measuring and verifying regeneration (not just sustainability)? |
| Integration with existing frameworks | How do our DO-1…DO-8 map onto existing reporting frameworks? |

### Category 6 — Governance & Social Infrastructure

| System | Question |
|---|---|
| Community energy governance models | What cooperative/co-governance models exist and have worked at scale? |
| Multi-stakeholder governance | Can we govern the intersection of farmer, energy company, community, regulator? |
| Benefit-sharing mechanisms | What legal/financial mechanisms enable transparent, fair benefit sharing? |
| Local MRV capacity | Does the local community/region have capacity to measure and verify outcomes independently? |

## 6. Method

For each enabling system, apply this assessment framework:

| Step | Output |
|---|---|
| **1. Map current state** | What exists today, in the relevant market(s)? |
| **2. Identify the gap** | What's missing for *our specific* use case? |
| **3. Classify** | Hard blocker / soft enabler / optional amplifier |
| **4. Map dependencies** | Which other enabling systems does it depend on? |
| **5. Assess feasibility** | Cost to create, time horizon, political/regulatory difficulty |
| **6. Recommend** | What the Task-Force needs to plan for, build, or wait on |

## 8. Deliverables

1. **Enabling systems map** — the full landscape across all six categories, with current-state assessment per system
2. **Gap analysis** — per system: exists / gap / needs creation, with feasibility score
3. **Classification matrix** — every system tagged as hard blocker / soft enabler / optional amplifier
4. **Dependency map** — which enabling systems depend on which others (graph or table)
5. **Critical path** — the minimum set of enabling systems that must exist for the thesis to hold
6. **Conditions for RQ2** — the answer to "better when…": under which combination of enabling systems does regenerative PVaaS win?
